## 51 detailed factors for proper strike

What happens in 1 tempo.


## Functions of Body Parts

### Feet

- Keep both feet facing forward.
- Keep the feet at a comfortable distance apart, allowing the body to move forward efficiently.

- Push with the ball of the left foot. Avoid pushing mainly with the toes or other parts of the foot, as they transfer power less effectively.
- Keep the right foot light rather than planted firmly on the floor, so it can move forward freely.

### Legs

- Keep the left leg straight to transfer power efficiently toward the center of gravity.
- Avoid bending or hyperextending the left knee, as either can disrupt power transfer.


### Center of Gravity (重心)

- Maintain a comfortable, balanced position that allows you to push the body forward easily.
- Imagine a vertical line running from the center of head, through the center of chest and then abdomen, to the floor between the feet.
- Keep the center of gravity centered rather than shifting left, right, forward, or backward.
- Keep the pelvis stable so it can receive power from the lower body and transfer it to the upper body.
- On each side of the body, maintain vertical alignment from the ear through the shoulder, pelvis, knee, and foot.

### Upper Body

- Keep the upper body fully relaxed. Excessive muscular tension can disrupt the transfer of power through the body.
- Let the chest rest naturally over the abdomen.
- Let the head rest naturally over the chest.

### Arms

- Think of the left arm as an extension of the shinai.
- Let both arms hang naturally at the sides of the body


### Fist and Grip

- Let the shinai rest naturally on the fingers of both hands.
- Keep the thenar eminence (大鱼际) over the top of the tsuka.
- Grip primarily with the little and ring fingers, with the middle finger applying light pressure.
- Avoid gripping with the thumb and index finger, as this can interfere with tenouchi.
- The thumb and index finger should still remain in natural contact with the tsuka for control and safety.
- Keep approximately one fist of space between the left and right hands, adjusting as needed for comfortable swinging and effective tenouchi.
- Avoid gripping tightly. Excessive tension can spread from the forearms to the upper arms and shoulders, disrupting the body's kinetic chain.
- Keep the left fist approximately one fist-width away from the body's centerline.

## Workflow

### distance

- Maintain *issoku-itto no maai* (一足一刀の間合い). Being too close or too far makes initiation more difficult because the body must first adjust to the distance.

## Cut

### Aiming

- Develop eye-hand coordination to guide the shinai accurately toward the target.

### Initial Movement

- Push with the left leg and let the power transfer through the torso, shoulder, and finally to the hands, especially the left fist.
- Keep this kinetic chain relaxed. Tension in any part of the chain can interrupt power transfer and waste energy.
- As the power reaches the left hand, a small amount of additional force may be used to initiate the upswing, but it is not always necessary.
- Think of the body as a large moving mass and the hands as smaller masses connected to it. When the body transfers force to the hands, their movement is constrained by the way they are connected to the body, naturally guiding them along a particular path and angle.
- Initiate with the left hand. The left hand travels along the inner arc, while the right hand travels along the outer arc.
- If the outer arc leads, the grip can become diagonal, making it harder to transfer power from the left hand to the shinai.
- Keep the force from the fist to the tsuka as nearly perpendicular for efficient power transfer
- Transfer force into the shinai through the base of the thumb, particularly the thenar eminence.
- As soon as the torso receives the force from the left leg, use that movement to drive the right leg forward.
- Let the right knee lead and the right foot follow naturally, it will keep the right foot directly below the right knee.


### Swing Up

- Use the power from the left leg to initiate the upswing, then add only enough force to let the shinai rise naturally.
- Maintain the basic kamae structure throughout the movement.
- Keep the upper arms moving primarily in a vertical plane.
- Continue the upswing until the left fist reaches an appropriate height for the downswing.

### Swing Down

- Initiate the downswing with the left fist and apply tenouchi. Different forms of tenouchi may be used.
- Accelerate the kensen first, giving it enough speed and momentum to travel naturally toward the target. This is the beginning of establishing proper hasuji (刃筋).
- Avoid initiating with the right hand. If the right hand leads, the left hand cannot transfer power efficiently, and the kensen may drift to the left.
- Stamp with the ball of the right foot rather than the heel to avoid excessive impact on the heel.
- As the right foot lands, use the movement to bring the left leg forward.
- Apply a brief push with the right fist near impact to further accelerate the kensen as part of tenouchi.

### Zanshin

- Relax unnecessary muscular tension immediately after striking the target.
- Maintain the hand and shinai position immediately after the cut while using footwork to move forward quickly.
- Keep the center of gravity at approximately the same height while moving.
- Coordinate the knees and feet to keep the body level. Avoid movement that causes the center of gravity to rise and fall unnecessarily.
- Keep both feet facing forward whenever possible so the ball of the foot can transfer force efficiently.
- Bring the left foot forward quickly and with a sufficiently large step to maximize forward speed.
- Maintain awareness of the opponent behind you as you complete zanshin.
- Because the hands remain in kamae, they only need to lower naturally.
- Turn efficiently toward the opponent by choosing the shorter direction of rotation.


## General Principles

- When changing the direction or phase of a movement, initiate the next motion with relaxed muscles rather than continuing to rely on the muscles just used.
- Maintain the current position unless there is a reason to change it. Avoiding unnecessary movement conserves energy and improves efficiency.
